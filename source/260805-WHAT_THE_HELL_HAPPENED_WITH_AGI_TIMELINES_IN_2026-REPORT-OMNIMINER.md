## ⚡ BOTTOM LINE
The surge in AI capabilities and revenue in 2026 suggests AGI timelines may have shortened by about a year, but significant uncertainties remain, especially regarding AI's ability to handle messy real‑world tasks and achieve recursive self‑improvement.

## 📝 THESIS
While AI has made striking progress in coding, revenue, and internal productivity, evidence indicates these gains are driven by scaling and reinforcement learning on high‑feedback tasks, not fundamental breakthroughs in general intelligence. The gap between clean, structured tasks and messy, open‑ended real‑world tasks persists, leaving AGI timelines uncertain.

## 💡 KEY INSIGHTS
1. **AI revenue is exploding** — Anthropic’s annualized revenue growth reached 8,400% over the last three months, implying a run‑rate of $47 bn in May 2026 and suggesting businesses are deriving real economic value from AI systems<sup>[1]</sup>
2. **Coding ability is accelerating fast** — The METR time‑horizon benchmark shows AI models completing software tasks that would take a human professional a day, with performance doubling roughly every four months, a pace far exceeding pre‑2024 trends<sup>[2]</sup>
3. **Mythos delivered a one‑off capability jump** — Anthropic’s Claude Mythos model produced a sudden six‑month leap in capabilities over three months, but progress reverted to the prior trend, indicating the jump came from training a larger model rather than a permanent acceleration<sup>[3]</sup>
4. **Internal AI use is boosting productivity** — Anthropic reports Claude now writes 80 % of its code, staff ship eight times more code, and the model suggests better research directions 64 % of the time, hinting at early recursive self‑improvement<sup>[4]</sup>
5. **AI still falters on messy real‑world tasks** — Experiments running cafés, vending‑machine simulations and the AI Village show models can handle structured tasks but struggle with open‑ended planning, execution and long‑term strategy, limiting their economic impact<sup>[5]</sup>
