<section class="wrap sources-section" id="sources"><div class="section-heading split"><div><p class="aside-label">Follow the sources</p><h2>Look closer.</h2></div><p class="deck">Methods, disagreements and limitations.<br>Open the question you want to investigate.</p></div><details id="feeding-sources"><summary>What do the feeding studies actually show?</summary><div>
<p><a href="https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0258044">Cavanaugh et al. (2021)</a>: reassuring short-term clinical, amino-acid and echocardiographic findings; small, uneven groups and withdrawals limit inference.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/microbiology/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2024.1367493/full">Guelph microbiome study (2024)</a>: 47 dogs analysed from the larger 61-completer randomised, double-blinded cohort. The <a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/veterinary-science/articles/10.3389/fvets.2025.1706965/full">2026 serum analysis</a> is another analysis of that trial, not another independent feeding experiment. Biological differences are not automatically health benefits.</p>
<p><a href="https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0298942">Linde et al. (2024)</a>: one year of clinical and nutritional monitoring in 15 healthy adult dogs. No concurrent control, unblinded examinations and one tested food limit generalisation. Cardiac biomarkers do not replace long-term cardiac surveillance.</p>
</div></details>
<details id="survey-sources"><summary>Why do survey headlines disagree?</summary><div>
<p>The <a href="https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0265662">2022 guardian survey</a> reported favourable associations. A <a href="https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0280173">May 2024 reanalysis</a> found negligible predictive contribution from vegan diet after modelling other variables. A <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e35578">September 2024 reanalysis</a> reported favourable associations; its full text was not verified for this guide, so no effect estimates are used.</p>
<p>These analyses reuse a self-selected observational dataset. They cannot establish that diet caused better health or longer life.</p>
</div></details>
<details id="quality-sources"><summary>Can reviews and laboratory tests settle adequacy?</summary><div>
<p>The <a href="https://doi.org/10.3390/vetsci10010052">2023 systematic review</a> found a small, generally low-certainty evidence base across 16 dog-and-cat studies. A <a href="https://doi.org/10.3390/ani16101454">May 2026 digestibility review</a> included 31 studies, only five assessing complete vegan or vegetarian diets via apparent total-tract digestibility. A <a href="https://doi.org/10.3390/ani16142232">July 2026 composition review</a> covered 54 pet-food studies, only six including vegan foods. Neither is a count of independent vegan health trials.</p>
<p>A <a href="https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0328506">2025 UK laboratory study</a> tested 31 dry foods purchased in 2022, including four vegan and two vegetarian products. Some plant foods fell short for iodine or B vitamins; other categories also had shortfalls. This historical sample is not a current brand ranking. Food composition and environmental modelling address different outcomes. Whether the 31-food laboratory study and the 31-food environmental model use identical samples was not established here; do not count them as independent replication.</p>
</div></details>
<details id="heart-sources"><summary>What about heart disease and peas or lentils?</summary><div>
<p>The <a href="https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/animal-health-literacy/questions-answers-fdas-work-potential-causes-non-hereditary-dcm-dogs">FDA's DCM investigation</a> describes a complex interaction of diet and other factors. Pulses appeared prominently in many reported foods; both grain-free and grain-containing foods were represented. It does not establish that vegan diets as a class cause DCM, or that every formulation is safe. A short trial or normal taurine result cannot settle every cardiac-risk question.</p>
</div></details>
<details id="method-sources"><summary>Definitions, method and interests</summary><div>
<p><strong>Vegan</strong> means no animal-derived ingredients. <strong>Plant-based</strong> can mean mostly plants; some research includes vegetarian foods containing dairy or eggs. A complete commercial food is different from an improvised home diet.</p>
<p>Targeted review of primary research, reviews and veterinary guidance through 22 September 2026. Not an exhaustive systematic review. We considered study design, sample, duration, repeated cohorts, formulation specificity and funding.</p>
<p>Interests exist across the literature: ProVeg funded the 2022 survey; a plant-based research initiative funded the one-year study; Guelph authors disclose industry relationships; a sceptical reanalysis author reports a <a href="https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0280173">Royal Canin-supported position</a> in the paper’s competing-interests declaration. Declarations inform scrutiny; they do not decide whether results are valid.</p>

</div></details>
